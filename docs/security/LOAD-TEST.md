# Load test: performance under attack

**No staff request failed, and the slowest 5% stayed under the limit, but response times did not meet the Week 4 example target of 10% slower.** During a flood of 4,387 attack requests from 50 addresses, 500 staff screen refreshes from six signed-in staff all succeeded, and the slowest 5% took 23.7 ms or less (limit 250 ms).

Generated by `npm run load-test -- --write` on 2026-11-09 (8 CPU threads, Node v24.19.0). It uses the real rate limits, real HTTP and a throwaway database, with the attack on its own thread. Staff and public traffic are served by separate app processes, as in production. Each figure is the round with the median 95th percentile, of 5 rounds of 100 requests. Timings vary with the machine's load, so run it on the production server for figures that count. Requirement SR-20.

| Measure | Staff alone | Staff during the attack |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Median response | 13.0 ms | 15.3 ms |
| 95th percentile | 19.2 ms | 23.7 ms |
| Failed requests | 0 of 500 | 0 of 500 |

**The attack:** 64 requests in flight at a time, from 50 addresses, mixing password guessing, booking-form spam and page loads. 416 of 4,387 attack requests (9%) were refused by the rate limits.

**Against the Week 4 example of "no more than 10% degradation":** the 95th percentile was 24% slower under attack, so it does not meet that target on this machine. The staff process still shares the machine and database with the flood. Staff were never refused or timed out.

**The limit of this result:** the flood here carries no session cookie, as a flood at a public site does. A flood that forges a session cookie reaches the staff process, where the rate limits still apply; a flood too large for the network connection needs protection in front of the server, such as Cloudflare (see OUT-OF-SCOPE.md).
